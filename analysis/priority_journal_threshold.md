# Priority-journal auto-include threshold — empirical evaluation

Generated by `analysis/priority_threshold_eval.py` on 2026-08-09. Zero network calls; all vectors from the single-user repo's caches (production Qwen3-Embedding-8B), relevance recomputed with the hosted app's exact `pipeline.shortlist.relevance_scores` recipe.

## Question

Papers from a user's priority journals get guaranteed judge slots, but only above a relevance baseline (owner: Journal of Communication is excellent but broad — auto-include must require 'at least pretty close' without cutting slightly-unique-but-interesting ones). Which baseline rule, at which level?

## Data

- July session: 41 rated papers joined to a reconstructed 47698-paper 14-day pool (doc_embeddings first_seen >= 2026-07-09); owner scale 0-2 mapped to 1/3/5.
- Aug session: 38 rated papers joined to the frozen 2086-candidate pool (free_stack/frozen_pool.json, 2026-08-04.. window); owner scale 1-5.
- Keep set (must retain >= 95%): rating >= 3 -> n=52. Strays: rating <= 2 -> n=27.
- Unjoinable (outside reconstructed window / no cached vector): July 0, Aug 0.

## Decision

**Winner: PCTL>=60** (retention 100.0%; 0/27 rated strays below the floor).

How to read the table: the rated packets were drawn from the TOP of each day's pool (judge-queue picks plus high-similarity bait), so nearly every rated paper — keeper or stray — sits above P90. The data therefore bounds the floor from above rather than discriminating between low floors:

- The lowest-percentile keeper is at pool percentile 83.5 (a rated-4 paper); the next ones are at ~P94-96 (two rated-5s). Floors of 85+ demonstrably start cutting papers the owner rated 4-5 — exactly the 'slightly-unique-but-interesting' papers the owner said must not be cut.
- Any floor at or below P80 retains 100% of the 52 keepers; we additionally require 20 points of headroom below that P83.5 minimum (=> floor <= 63), because the July pool is a reconstruction, production runs a different embedder (nemotron-3-embed-1b vs these Qwen3-8B vectors), and other users' seed geometries will differ.
- P60 is the highest grid level satisfying both — also the top of the owner's suggested 40/50/60 candidate range. It still blocks the bottom 60% of a broad journal's output from consuming guaranteed judge slots, while the per-user cap (PRIORITY_JOURNAL_MAX_PER_USER) bounds quota regardless.

Note on rated strays: the packet strays are non-representative of a broad journal's typical off-topic paper — they were selected into packets *because* they embed near the seeds (bait). A journal's genuinely off-topic papers sit far lower in the pool distribution and are exactly what any P40-P80 floor blocks.

## Why a percentile rule (not an absolute floor)

- Absolute cosine floors do NOT transfer across embedders: these vectors are Qwen3-Embedding-8B, production papersradar runs nemotron-3-embed-1b (and a local Qwen3-0.6B swap is planned). A percentile of the user's own windowed pool is scale-free and survives any embedder change untouched.
- It is also per-user by construction: a user with a tight seed cloud and a user with a broad one both get 'above the bottom X% of MY day's pool', which is what 'at least pretty close' means in each one's geometry.

## Encoded as

`PRIORITY_JOURNAL_MIN_REL_PCTL=60` (config knob, see app/config.py) — a priority-journal paper is auto-queued for judging only if its relevance is at or above that percentile of the user's windowed pool that day, capped at `PRIORITY_JOURNAL_MAX_PER_USER` extra slots.

Caveats: the July pool is a cache-based reconstruction (the exact harvest pool wasn't frozen); both pools are the owner's — other users' geometries will differ, which the percentile form absorbs.
