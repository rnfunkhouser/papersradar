About Research Radar

Research Radar exists because keeping up with new research shouldn't come down to luck. Keyword alerts are imprecise — my Google Scholar alerts buried the occasional right paper under a pile of near-misses, and still managed to miss work I genuinely needed to see. Too often, the best new paper in my area reached me only because a colleague happened to post about it, and I happened to catch the post.

I wanted a tool that reads each day's new papers the way a knowledgeable colleague would — judging whether a paper actually fits what you study, not whether it shares a few words with your search terms — and that shows its reasoning for every pick so you can tune it. That's what Research Radar tries to be.

I'm Ryan Funkhouser, a political-communication researcher at the University of Idaho, where I study how communication shapes political attitudes and behavior.

Research Radar is a free tool, and I've developed it in part using AI. I welcome feedback, feature requests, bug reports, or any other notes at admin@papersradar.com.

If you find this a useful service and want to help with the basic costs of maintaining it — or otherwise support the project — you can buy me a coffee ☕. Appreciated, never expected.
